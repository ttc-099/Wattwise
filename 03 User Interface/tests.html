How the estimate works

CREAM makes the aggregator send each buyer's share of solar 120 days before the month happens (Skrine, 2025; Energy Commission, 2025), long before anyone knows how much that buyer will use. WattWise has to make that call four months ahead. Here is the method one step at a time, with live numbers you can change, and then how we tested it.

1Read2Estimate3Allocate4Send5Settle6Test
1
ReadThree files go in. Nothing else.
  • Buyers' monthly meter readingsbuyer, month, MWh · the history every estimate starts from
  • Splits already allocatedwhat was sent in past months, to compare against
  • Scheme solar outputwhat the roofs generate each month

No hardware and no connection to the grid. In the prototype only the meter-readings file is actually parsed; the splits and solar files are demo placeholders that the engine does not read yet. This page uses the same demo readings (synthetic, not real meters) so the numbers below match the operator app.

2
EstimateOne rule, the same for every buyer.
estimate = same month last year × (1 + trend)

Same month last year, because use follows the seasons: a mall's March looks like last March, not like last October. Trend, because buyers grow or shrink: the latest 3 complete months against the same 3 months a year earlier, kept within ±25%. The operator app reads it from the meter file and the replay uses the same rule. The demo file repeats one year, so its trend is 0; move the slider to see what a trend does.

What the estimator is allowed to see

meter reading on filethe month it usesnot happened yet when the share is sentthe month being planned

What it does not use No weather, no machine learning, and no data from after the release date. It is a rule anyone can check by hand. Step 6 shows how it does against the alternatives.
3
AllocateSize each share to its estimate, keep a slice back.
share = estimate ÷ the larger of expected solar and all estimates × (1 − hold-back)

Expected solar is the scheme's size times the long-term rooftop yield at Elmina for that month (European Commission JRC, 2022). If the buyers are expected to use less than the roofs make, each gets its estimate and the rest is held back too; if they are expected to use more, the shares are scaled down. Whatever is not allocated is sold via NEDA.

Why hold some back? The two mistakes do not cost the same. Allocating a kWh a buyer cannot use loses about 17 sen: the 8 sen NEDA sale it could have been, plus the 9 sen access charge still paid on it (Energy Commission, 2025, clauses 9.7, 9.9 and 13.2). Holding back a kWh the buyer would have used loses the CREAM price minus 17 sen: 13 sen at an assumed 30 sen, nothing at 17 sen. So a small hold-back pays when the price is low and costs a little when it is high. The replay and the operator app use 2%.

4
SendStamped with its 120-day notice date, approved by a person.
shares sentthe month happensAfterTNB reads the meters

WattWise suggests the split. The scheme admin approves it. SDP files with the Energy Commission. Each buyer gets a notice email with a PDF, which the operator can preview before anything goes out. WattWise never sends on its own.

5
SettleCheck the share against what the buyer really used.
given away = allocated − used, when allocated is more

Illustrative numbers. In the replay, a real two-buyer scheme at Elmina's size gave away 557.3 MWh in a year under a fixed split and 132.0 MWh under WattWise; Appendix A shows every month. Each settled month also feeds the next estimate: new readings arrive and the basis moves forward a month.

6
TestDoes the rule beat what people do today? Replayed on real meters.
  1. Real use. Monthly electricity from 821 real buildings at 11 sites, 2016 to 2017 (Miller et al., 2020). Each building is a one-buyer scheme; buildings at the same site are paired into 407 two-buyer schemes.
  2. Real solar. Rooftop solar at Elmina, hour by hour for 2017 (European Commission JRC, 2022), sized so the expected yearly solar equals the buyers' use the year before.
  3. Same constraint as CREAM. Every month of 2017 is planned 120 days ahead, using only readings that existed at that point.
  4. Three ways to set the split, on the same buildings: a fixed annual split (what happens today), copy last year (the same month last year, as is), and WattWise (this page).
  5. Measures: the share of the year's solar given away (lower is better), and the scheme's income at 15 to 30 sen/kWh.

Where it is weak

A median month's estimate is off by 7.9%, and a buyer's worst month by 22.4% (median) at the four-month distance (WattWise, 2026). On money, copying last year does slightly better than WattWise once the CREAM price is 25 sen or more (-RM1.39 per MWh at 30 sen), because the hold-back sells at 8 sen some energy a buyer would have used. Perfect foresight would give away 0%; we use it only as a ceiling, never as a claim.

Appendix B · Do homeowners really earn that much?

The CREAM price is private, so every RM figure is modelled at an assumed 15, 20, 25 and 30 sen/kWh. Each figure is revenue(WattWise) − revenue(fixed split) for the same year, where revenue = used kWh × price + NEDA kWh × 8 sen − 9 sen × (solar − NEDA kWh) (Energy Commission, 2025, clauses 9.7, 9.9 and 13.2).

CLAIMHomeowners earn from leasing their roofs (Sime Darby Property, 2025), and WattWise adds to the scheme's income.
TESTTake the median extra income per MWh of solar across the 407 real two-buyer schemes, apply it to Elmina's size (9,409 MWh a year), then divide across 1,368 illustrative roofs.
RESULTAt the 20 to 30 sen price band: RM24,600 (at 30 sen) to RM60,400 (at 20 sen) a year at Elmina's modelled size, RM18 to RM44 per roof (modelled); RM108k to RM265k at 6,000 roofs. WattWise earned more than a fixed split in 95.3% of schemes at 20 sen, and 70.5% at 30 sen.
VERDICTPASS, WITH CAVEATS · real but modest per roof, and smaller the higher the CREAM price. Lease terms are negotiated, not set by this tool.

Why a bigger scheme stays safe

A fixed split hands out the same share whatever the month looks like, so extra solar spills straight to the grid. WattWise sizes each month to the buyers' expected use and holds the rest back for NEDA, so most of the extra solar is sold instead of given away.

Compliance

One allocation per buyer per month. Changes to a buyer's percentage must be made in writing no later than 120 days before they take effect, and take effect only on the 1st of a month (Energy Commission, 2025, clause 9.10(e)). WattWise suggests, the scheme admin approves, SDP files.

Estimate accuracy behind the wins: a median month is off by 7.9%, a buyer's worst month by 22.4% (WattWise, 2026). The bench replays real monthly meter readings from the Building Data Genome Project 2 (821 buildings, 2016 to 2017), at the same 120-day distance CREAM requires.
