How the estimate works

CREAM makes the aggregator send each buyer's share of solar 120 days before the month happens (Skrine, 2025; Energy Commission, 2025), long before anyone knows how much that buyer will use. WattWise has to make that call four months ahead. Here is the method one step at a time, with live numbers you can change, and then how we tested it.

1Read2Estimate3Allocate4Send5Settle6Test
1
ReadThree files go in. Nothing else.
  • Buyers' monthly meter readingsbuyer, month, MWh · the history every estimate starts from
  • Splits already allocatedwhat was sent in past months, to compare against
  • Scheme solar outputwhat the roofs generate each month

No hardware and no connection to the grid. In the prototype only the meter-readings file is actually parsed; the splits and solar files are demo placeholders that the engine does not read yet. This page uses the same demo readings (synthetic, not real meters) so the numbers below match the operator app.

2
EstimateOne rule, the same for every buyer.
estimate = same month last year × (1 + trend)

Same month last year, because use follows the seasons: a mall's March looks like last March, not like last October. Trend, because buyers grow or shrink: a small yearly nudge, up or down. In this prototype the trend is a per-buyer input the operator can edit (the demo uses fixed defaults of −2% to +2%); it is not yet calculated from the readings, and the benchmark's trend rule is not in the repository.

What the estimator is allowed to see

meter reading on filethe month it usesnot happened yet when the share is sentthe month being planned

What it does not use No weather, no machine learning, and no data from after the release date. It is a rule anyone can check by hand. Step 6 shows it already beats the alternatives.
3
AllocateSplit the solar in proportion to the estimates, keep a slice back.
share = estimate ÷ all estimates × (1 − hold-back)

Why hold some back? The two mistakes do not cost the same. Allocating too much hands solar to a buyer who cannot use it, and the scheme loses about 17 sen for every kWh (WattWise, 2026). Allocating too little costs far less, because the held-back energy is still sold via NEDA at 8 sen/kWh, with no access charge (Energy Commission, 2025, clauses 9.7 and 13.2). The Commission can change that rate, so every RM figure is modelled. So a small, deliberate hold-back is cheap insurance against a bad guess.

4
SendStamped with its 120-day notice date, approved by a person.
shares sentthe month happensAfterTNB reads the meters

WattWise suggests the split. The scheme admin approves it. SDP files with the Energy Commission. Each buyer gets a notice email with a PDF, which the operator can preview before anything goes out. WattWise never sends on its own.

5
SettleCheck the share against what the buyer really used.
given away = allocated − used, when allocated is more

Illustrative numbers. In the simulated Elmina year a fixed split gave away 434.2 MWh and WattWise 115.3 MWh; Appendix A shows it month by month. Each settled month also feeds the next estimate: new readings arrive and the basis moves forward a month.

6
TestDoes the rule beat what people do today? Replayed on real meters.

  2. Same constraint as CREAM. Every month is planned four months ahead, using only readings that existed at that point.
  3. Three ways to set the split, replayed on the same buildings: a fixed annual split (what happens today), copy last year (the same month last year, used as is), and WattWise (this page).
  4. One measure: the share of the year's solar given away. Lower is better.

Where it is weak

The worst month's estimate is off by 24% (one-buyer) to 32% (two-buyer) at the four-month distance, and 40% in Malaysia-like two-buyer schemes (WattWise, 2026). WattWise still wins over a year because over-allocating costs far more than under-allocating, and the hold-back takes the hit first. Perfect foresight would give away 0%; we use it only as a ceiling, never as a claim.

Why a bigger scheme stays safe

A fixed split hands out the same share whatever the month looks like, so extra solar spills straight to the grid. WattWise sizes each month to the buyers' expected use and holds the rest back for NEDA, so most of the extra solar is sold instead of given away.

Compliance

Allocation cadence: one allocation per buyer per month, each stamped with its 120-day notice date. WattWise suggests, the scheme admin approves, SDP files with the Energy Commission. (Energy Commission, 2025) (verbatim clause quote pending).

Estimate accuracy behind the wins: worst month's error 24–40% (WattWise, 2026). The bench replays real monthly meter readings from the Building Data Genome Project 2 (590 buildings, 2016 to 2017), at the same 4-month distance CREAM requires.
